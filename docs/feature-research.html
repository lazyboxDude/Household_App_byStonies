Feature Research: Homsy, Sweepy, Bring!

Analysis of three household apps to inform our roadmap. Findings feed the TODO list below so we know what's next. Status below was checked against the code on 2026-10-05. The section Idea: Routinen covers recurring chores and bills (Abfuhr, Stromrechnung, …): phases 1–3 are built and their migrations are applied, phase 4 is a proposal.

Homsy (household/family organizer)

  • Chores scheduled daily/weekly/monthly, auto-rotating between household members.
  • Tasks assigned to a specific person, who gets notified and sees it on their own list.
  • Completion streaks per person to keep contribution visible and fair.
  • One app covering chores + grocery list + meal planner + shared calendar + expense tracker.
  • Works offline, syncs when back online; each family member has their own login.

Sweepy (cleaning schedule)

  • Setup flow: add each room in the house, then add tasks per room, each with its own frequency and a starting “cleanliness state”.
  • Room-level cleanliness indicator (not just a flat task list) — you see which rooms are neglected at a glance.
  • Auto-generates a daily schedule per person from all room tasks combined.
  • Prioritizes tasks that are most overdue / most urgent.
  • Gamification: coins for completed tasks, spendable on a virtual home; group leaderboard.

Bring! (shopping list)

  • Real-time shared list sync + push notifications when someone checks off / adds an item.
  • Automatic categorization of items into aisles, with user-customizable categories.
  • Recipe “templates” that add a whole ingredient list to the shopping list in one tap.
  • Voice control via Siri/Alexa.
  • Loyalty cards stored in-app; light/dark mode and tile/list view toggle.

Idea: Routinen — one rhythm for chores and bills

Phase 1 built · phases 2–4 proposal

The problem

A household runs on things that come back: Kehricht-Abfuhr every second Thursday, Papiersammlung on fixed dates, the Strom-Abschlag on the 1st, the Jahresabrechnung in March, Serafe every quarter, the bathroom every week. Today the app handles recurrence in three places that don't talk to each other:

  • Cleaning Plan — cleaning_tasks.next_due, interval-only, “after done” logic, monthly = 30 days.
  • Rechnungen-Planer — verteilertopf_bills.months[], a funding forecast only; no due date, payer, split or “paid”.
  • General tasks — no recurrence at all.

Abfuhr doesn't fit any of them: it is fixed to the calendar, costs nothing, and the question is only “who puts the bin out tonight?”.

The idea in one sentence

A Routine is something that comes back on a schedule. Every routine answers three questions — When? Who? How much? — and the app turns the answers into one shared “Diese Woche” view, calendar entries and (for bills) expenses.

A chore, a reminder and a bill are the same thing with different parts switched on:

  • Reminder (Abfuhr, Papiersammlung): When ✓, Who optional, How much —
  • Chore (Bad putzen, Pflanzen giessen): When ✓, Who ✓ (fixed, rotating, open), How much —
  • Bill (Strom, Internet, Miete, Serafe): When ✓, Who pays ✓, How much ✓ and how it is split

When — schedules

The Cleaning Plan only knows “every N days after last done”. Real households need more, and they need two different anchoring modes:

Schedule typeReal-world exampleMode
Every N days/weeks/months/yearsStaubsaugen every 7 days; Serafe every 3 monthsafter done or fixed
Weekday, optionally every N-th weekKehricht every second Thursdayfixed
Day of month (or “last day”), optionally only some monthsStrom-Abschlag on the 1st; Jahresabrechnung in Marchfixed
N-th weekday of the monthKartonsammlung: first Mondayfixed
Explicit date listEntsorgungskalender of the Gemeinde (imported once a year)fixed
Active months (season)Grünabfuhr only March–Novemberfixed

Fixed (calendar-bound) vs. after done (relative to completion) is the key distinction. Abfuhr and bills are fixed: if you miss Thursday, the next one is still in two weeks. Cleaning is after-done: if you vacuum late, the next round starts from then. The existing Cleaning Plan is the after-done special case. Date lists and weekday/month-day schedules are fixed-only.

How much — bills

  • Amount kind: fixed (Miete, Internet), estimate (Strom-Abschlag, can be corrected when the real bill arrives) or variable (entered when paid).
  • Payer: who pays it to the outside world (invoice stays with one person, or comes out of the joint account).
  • Split built: percentages per member, equal or custom (WG: Internet equal, Heizung by room size). A copy of the split is stored on the occurrence when it is paid, so later changes do not rewrite history. By income share is not built (see TODO).
  • Flow built: due → tap “Bezahlt” → actual amount → optionally an expenses row for the person paying (same pattern as the shopping check-off) and optionally a debit on a Verteilertopf account (verteilertopf_tx, kind “expense”). For estimate/variable bills the paid amount can become the amount for the next due dates, which is what the forecast uses. Everything is undoable for 8 seconds. The follow-up “Strom: Jahresabrechnung prüfen” (Nachzahlung oder Guthaben?) is a reminder template, not an automatic link.
  • Settle-up built: “Wer schuldet wem?” is computed from the paid bills that have a split (whoever is marked as having paid is owed the others' shares) minus recorded paybacks, and suggests the fewest transfers; “Ausgeglichen” records a payback. It only appears once a split or payback exists, so couples who pay from the joint account never see it.

Signature features

  1. “Diese Woche” built, without a “mine” filter — one agenda mixing chores, Abfuhr and bills for the next 7 days (a bill shows as far ahead as its “Vorher Bescheid sagen”; unpaid bills stay until paid or skipped). Doubles as the dashboard card.

  2. Evening-before reminder built as in-app highlight (lead_days) — “Morgen Kehricht — Tonne rausstellen”. In-app banner first; real push once the service worker exists (see Later).
  3. Swap & absence built — one tap “tauschen” hands an occurrence to someone else; absence ranges skip a person in rotations.
  4. Fairness-Waage built — share of effort (effort 1–5 per routine) over the last 30 days against a target split (50/50, or 40/60 for part-time). No points, no leaderboard by default.
  5. Entsorgungskalender import phase 4; manual date list built — paste or upload the municipality's ICS/CSV once a year and get all Abfuhr dates as a date-list routine.
  6. Starter pack built as form templates, not in onboarding yet — in onboarding, tick the typical routines (Kehricht, Karton, Strom, Internet, Miete, Serafe, Krankenkasse, Putzplan). The money onboarding's fixed costs can be offered as bill routines instead of only budgets.
  7. Calendar built — every open occurrence is mirrored into calendar_events through a new unique source_occurrence_id (one entry per occurrence, removed when it is done or skipped, restored on undo). Only when the Calendar feature is on and the routine has “Im Kalender anzeigen”.

Data model sketch

Phases 1–2 build the subset below without effort, rotation, fair_share, split and isShared (and expenseId/vtTxId are stored on the occurrence); the real types are in app/tasks/routines/types.ts. Weekdays use JS numbering (0 = Sunday).

type RoutineKind = "chore" | "reminder" | "bill";

type Schedule =
  | { type: "interval"; every: number; unit: "day" | "week" | "month" | "year"; anchor: string }
  | { type: "weekday"; weekdays: number[]; everyNWeeks?: number; anchor?: string }
  | { type: "monthday"; day: number | "last"; months?: number[] }
  | { type: "nth_weekday"; nth: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | -1; weekday: number; months?: number[] }
  | { type: "dates"; dates: string[] };            // imported Entsorgungskalender

interface Routine {
  id: string;
  householdId: string;
  kind: RoutineKind;
  title: string;
  icon: string;
  schedule: Schedule;
  mode: "fixed" | "after_done";    // after_done only with type "interval"
  activeMonths?: number[];         // season, e.g. Grünabfuhr 3–11
  leadDays: number;                // remind N days before
  isShared: boolean;               // false = "Only me"

  // Who
  assignment: "fixed" | "rotation" | "fair_share" | "open";
  assigneeId?: string;             // user id (fixed)
  rotation?: string[];             // user ids in order
  effort: 1 | 2 | 3 | 5;
  roomId?: string;                 // keeps the Cleaning Plan link

  // How much (kind = "bill")
  amount?: number;
  amountKind?: "fixed" | "estimate" | "variable";
  payerId?: string;
  split?: Record<string, number>;  // user id -> percent, sums to 100
  expenseCategory?: string;
}

interface Occurrence {
  id: string;
  routineId: string;
  dueDate: string;                 // ISO date
  status: "open" | "done" | "skipped";
  assignedTo?: string;
  doneBy?: string;
  doneAt?: string;
  actualAmount?: number;
  expenseId?: string;              // created on "bezahlt"
}

Occurrences are materialised for the next 8 weeks (extended whenever the Tasks page or dashboard is opened; the unique (routine_id, due_date) makes simultaneous opens safe) rather than computed on the fly: that keeps history for the fairness view and lets a single occurrence be swapped, skipped or paid with a different amount without touching the routine.

How it fits the existing code

  • Schedule engine — built as pure functions in app/tasks/routines/schedule.ts (occurrencesBetween, nextAfterDone, date-list parser) with 26 tests in schedule.test.ts; real month arithmetic, all dates calculated in UTC to avoid time-zone shifts. Still needs npm test in CI to be worth anything.
  • Calendar — Routinen sync lives in useRoutines.ts, separate from the Cleaning Plan's calendarSync.ts; deletion works through DB cascade.
  • Rechnungen-Planer built as merge — billPlan.ts reduces each bill routine to “amount, due months” from its real due dates over the next 12 months and useVerteilertopf adds them to the manual list for Soll-Stand, monthly need and the forecast. They show in a read-only “Aus Routinen” card. The yearly total is exact; the per-month amount is an average for bills due more than once a month.
  • Cleaning Plan — stays as the room view. Later its tasks become routines with a roomId; until then both coexist.
  • Members — routines use profile ids (assignee_id, rotation[], payer_id, split keys), so rotation, fairness and splits work on real members. The older Tasks list and Cleaning Plan keep their free-text assignee.
  • Planning who does what — rotation.ts is pure and deterministic: it only plans occurrences that are open, ahead and not swapped by hand, continues from the last person who had a turn, and only writes what changed, so several devices agree. Absences or member changes rerun it.
  • Migrations — the new tables need RLS via is_household_member; this is a good moment to add the missing migrations folder to the repo.

Data model sketch (Cleaning Plan)

type Recurrence = "daily" | "weekly" | "biweekly" | "monthly" | "once";

interface Room {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  icon: string; // lucide icon key
}

interface CleaningTask {
  id: string;
  roomId: string;
  title: string;
  supplies: string[];   // e.g. ["All-purpose cleaner", "Microfiber cloth"]
  recurrence: Recurrence;
  assignee?: string;
  lastDone?: string;    // ISO date
  nextDue: string;      // ISO date, derived from lastDone + recurrence
}

Each cleaning task's next_due is mirrored into the shared calendar_events table (column source_cleaning_task_id, title prefixed with 🧹, 09:00) when the task is created and recomputed whenever it is marked done. Deleting a task or room removes its events via DB cascade. The Calendar page does not treat these events specially.
